The problem

Guarantees lock up cash. Trade credit breaks when one link fails.

Rent upfrontIn Lagos, tenants pay one to two years in advance. Landlords want certainty, so capital sits idle.
Merchant depositsSuppliers and platforms demand cash deposits or bonds that earn nothing and can't be reused.
IOU chainsShops owe each other in loops. One default and everyone downstream is short, even when the loop nets to almost zero.
Par02
The insight

A MUSD trove's undrawn capacity is a fixed, BTC-backed credit line. Par lets you promise it.

Lock BTC once. Reserve capacity against commitments. Nothing is drawn until a promise is called, and then the vault pays automatically.

0MUSD drawn per commitment until it's called
−40%BTC drop every promise must survive before it exists
160%Target ratio at the stressed price, everything called at once
1 txKeeper or anyone triggers a payout; no approval needed
Par03
How it works

Vault → trove → commitments → payout waterfall

VaultAn EIP-1167 clone you own. It is the borrower of record of one MUSD trove.
→
TroveBTC collateral; capacity and rate locked at open. 1,800 MUSD minimum kept as a buffer.
→
CommitmentsInstallment guarantees, claim bonds, Circuit clearing lines. Each one is stress-tested.
→
PayoutBuffer first, then mint MUSD. In Recovery Mode, top up with the vault's idle BTC.
Par04
Product 1 · Par Guarantees

Rent, supplier terms and bonds that pay themselves.

Installment guaranteeThe tenant pays monthly. Miss one, and after the grace period the vault pays the landlord, up to the cover cap.
Claim bondThe beneficiary files a claim with evidence. Undisputed claims pay after the window; disputes go to an independent arbiter with a 7-day deadline.
Live certificateA shareable page (WhatsApp preview) showing cover left, expiry and how far BTC can fall before cover is at risk. Verifiable onchain.
Par05
Product 2 · Par Circuit

Trade credit that clears itself.

Members invoice each other. ParCircuit nets every accepted invoice in O(1). At cycle end only net debtors pay; a defaulter is force-settled from the clearing line pledged in their vault.

2,900MUSD of invoices in the demo circle
100MUSD that actually had to move
96.55%netted. The defaulter was force-settled by the keeper, on testnet, with no human in the loop.
Par06
Mezo integration

Built on MUSD's internals, not on top of a token.

MUSD primitiveHow Par uses it
BorrowerOperationsEach vault opens and manages its own trove: openTrove, withdrawMUSD, adjustTrove, repay, refinance, claimCollateral
TroveManager + PriceFeedCapacity snapshot, ICR / system TCR, Recovery Mode, MCR 110% and CCR 150% enforced before every draw
HintHelpers / SortedTrovesKeeper computes insert hints for every draw
MUSD tokenAll guarantees, invoices, settlements and payouts are denominated and paid in MUSD

Every Par promise is a new MUSD trove holding BTC, and every called promise mints MUSD. It extends MUSD's utility from borrowing to guaranteeing and clearing.

Business

Who it's for, and how it makes money.

UsersBTC holders who want their Bitcoin to work without selling it; tenants, merchants and SMEs in markets where guarantees cost cash (starting with Lagos).
Revenue0.5–1% a year on cover issued, plus 5–10 bps on Circuit gross volume cleared. Both off during the hackathon.
For MezoNew troves, more BTC locked, MUSD as the unit of account for rent and trade credit, and a reason to hold MUSD in real economies.
